WHY NOW

The quantum threat is a migration problem, not a science problem

Shor's algorithm breaks RSA, ECC and Diffie-Hellman. Even if large-scale quantum computers arrive later, adversaries can record encrypted traffic today and decrypt it retroactively - the "harvest now, decrypt later" threat. Data with long confidentiality lifetimes must be protected now.

THREAT

Harvest now, decrypt later

Encrypted communications and stored data intercepted today can be decrypted once cryptographically relevant quantum computers exist. Long-lived secrets - credentials, health records, state secrets, financial archives - are already at risk.

STANDARD

NIST post-quantum standards are here

NIST finalised FIPS 203 (ML-KEM key encapsulation), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA signatures) and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA stateless signatures) in 2024. The transition window is open - organisations that wait inherit emergency migrations.

COMPLEXITY

PQC is an ecosystem migration

Algorithms are only part of it. Certificates, protocols, hardware, firmware, libraries and operational processes all embed classical assumptions. Inventory and agility matter more than any single algorithm choice.

PQC FUNDAMENTALS

The NIST-standardised post-quantum family

Three FIPS standards define the first generation of quantum-resistant cryptography.

ML-KEM (FIPS 203)

Module-Lattice Key Encapsulation - the standard for quantum-safe key exchange, successor to Diffie-Hellman/RSA key transport. Used for hybrid TLS key establishment.

ML-DSA (FIPS 204)

Module-Lattice Digital Signature - the primary quantum-safe signature standard for certificates, firmware signing, code signing and document integrity.

SLH-DSA (FIPS 205)

Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature - conservative, conservative-assumption signatures for long-lived signatures, root-of-trust and high-assurance scenarios where signature lifetime matters most.

Honest framing: PQC algorithms are new and will be scrutinised for decades. Defensible architectures deploy them in hybrid mode alongside classical cryptography - so security never depends on a single unproven assumption.

QKD & QUANTUM COMMUNICATIONS

Quantum key distribution - physics-based key exchange

QKD uses quantum mechanics - typically photon polarisation over optical fibre - to detect eavesdropping during key establishment. It is complementary to PQC: QKD protects the key-exchange channel physically; PQC protects the mathematics of the algorithms themselves.

Where QKD fits

Metro-scale fibre links between fixed sites, government and defence backbone encryption, and high-assurance data-centre interconnects where physical-layer guarantees are worth the infrastructure cost.

Where it does not

QKD requires dedicated fibre and trusted endpoints - it does not replace PQC for general internet traffic, certificates or software. It authenticates nothing by itself; the classical channel still needs PQC or pre-shared keys.

MIGRATION METHODOLOGY

How a PQC transition runs

  1. Cryptographic discovery - inventory every algorithm, certificate, key and protocol; map data flows and lifetimes.
  2. Risk prioritisation - rank assets by harvest-now-decrypt-later exposure and migration cost.
  3. Target architecture - hybrid PQC design, crypto-agility layer, certificate strategy and HSM/KMS integration.
  4. Pilot deployment - hybrid TLS and signing in non-critical paths; measure performance and interoperability.
  5. Phased migration - certificates, protocols, firmware and applications moved in risk order with rollback plans.
  6. Assurance - ongoing inventory, algorithm monitoring and readiness for future standards and QKD integration.
